Scale II · 40,962 people on a world

Zero-points in a population

Scale III · one field filling space

Knots made of stillness

Let the population become a continuum filling three dimensions. The zero-states are no longer points but closed lines, and the waves of reaching and resisting turn around them. These lines can tie into knots and link through each other. Under positive feedback they tighten, reconnect and vanish. Under negative feedback the zero-state swells out from them.

What emerged

Running the wave model

The paper describes knots, a mirror, an ego that switches feedback, and a zero-state. Written as equations and run, those four ideas make one specific dynamical system. At the scale of a population or a universe it produces geometry the paper doesn’t mention: points and lines of stillness that come in pairs, knot together, and act as the seeds from which release spreads.

The knot law f(z) = (μ + iω)z + (1 + iβ)|z|²z − |z|⁴z is the normal form of a subcritical Hopf bifurcation, the simplest equation in which an oscillation can switch on abruptly and then refuse to switch off. I chose it because it is the smallest law that reproduces the paper’s claims together: amplification, a threshold, a flip after release, and release that needs several cycles.

1The ego works as a bifurcation parameter

For μ > 0 every knot, however small, grows to the attachment cycle. For −¼ < μ < 0 the zero-state and attachment coexist: small knots dissolve, large ones persist. Below μ = −¼ every knot dissolves. None of the paper’s behaviours had to be added by hand. A compensation that overshoots rotates the knot past 90°, so a reaching knot comes back as a smaller resisting one (Fig. 9). A deep knot pushed only part of the way down climbs back to attachment, so it needs several cycles (Accumulation section). There is also hysteresis: once a knot has reached zero, it stays there until μ climbs all the way back above 0.

2Aim matters more than dose

An antidote multiplies the knot by 1 − αeiδ. It helps only when cos δ > α/2. A full-strength antidote (α = 1) aimed more than 60° away from the root leaves the knot larger than before. Gentler doses tolerate more error, which fits the paper’s observation from exposure therapy that release is easier at low intensity. This is the arithmetic behind its point that finding the root cause matters most.

3Release arrives as a cascade

Each release lowers μ and sharpens aim, which makes the next release easier. That feedback makes lives in the model hold at attachment for a long time and then fall through the threshold in a short burst, rather than decline gradually. With the default settings, 76 of 200 simulated lives reached the zero-state, at a median of 81% of the lifespan; the rest died still attached. Run 200 lives on the Psyche tab reproduces this, and shows how the settings move the split. Seen from above, a knot’s track spirals outward while it accumulates, zigzags across the axis as antidotes arrive, and settles onto the straight line of the axis: the loops, zigzag and line of the unalome the paper mentions.

4Zero-states become topological defects

When psyches are coupled to their neighbours, a person’s phase has to vary smoothly across the population. Around some closed loops the phase winds once through reaching, rising, resisting and falling. A continuous field that winds like that must pass through z = 0 inside the loop, so a zero-point sits there. It cannot be removed by any local change. Zero-points come in two handednesses and are created and destroyed only in opposite pairs, so on a closed world the net charge is always exactly zero. Your duality of reaching and resisting shows up at this scale as a conservation law. The spiral waves on the planet are everyone’s knots circulating around these still points.

5Release spreads from the zero-points

This was the result I didn’t expect. Take a world in the bistable band (−¼ < μ < 0). If everyone is in sync and there is no zero-point anywhere, attachment holds all the way down to μ = −¼, because the zero-state has nowhere to start. If the world contains zero-points, the zero-state grows outward from them and takes over the planet at a much higher μ. How much higher depends on the rhythm shift β. With β = 0, release starts near μ ≈ −0.2, close to the Maxwell point −3/16 where the two states are equally stable. From β ≈ 0.75 upward, any negative gain is enough. The zero-points act as nucleation sites. A population with a variety of phases can reach the zero-state at a higher ego gain than a synchronised one.

6A fast birth of the ego leaves more still points

Start a population at zero and ramp μ through 0 over a time τ: the ego switching on across a whole society. Separate regions choose their phases independently, and zero-points freeze in where the choices disagree. Measured over six ramp times from 32 to 1024 units, the number of zero-points left behind falls as τ−0.52. Mean-field theory predicts τ−½. This is the Kibble–Zurek law, first worked out for defects frozen into the early universe and later checked in superfluid helium. A fast, disordered birth leaves a world with more nucleation sites for release.

7In three dimensions, the knots are made of stillness

In a field filling space, the zero-set is a set of closed curves, and those curves can knot and link. Seeding the field so that the zero-set is two linked rings gives a Gauss linking number of −1.000 when measured on the simulated lines, and a trefoil seed gives a single knotted loop. So the paper’s knots come back at the largest scale as literal knots, but made of zero-state, with the waves of reaching and resisting circulating around them. Under positive feedback the lines behave like strings under tension: they tighten, reconnect and disappear, leaving a uniform field with no stillness in it. Reconnection is how these knots untie. The Hopf link merges into one loop within a few time units, then splits into two unlinked rings, and its linking number drops from −1 to 0. Under negative feedback the zero-state grows outward along the lines as tubes and fills the space. A uniform field with no lines, at the same μ = −0.05, doesn’t change.

The same equation governs spiral waves in the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction and in heart tissue, quantised vortex lines in superfluids, and the cosmic strings proposed for the early universe. The paper cites the Hermetic principle “as above, so below”. Here it has a precise form: near the point where an oscillation switches on, any system of waves with feedback and neighbours reduces to this equation, whatever the waves are made of.

Which parts are my modelling choices

  • The paper doesn’t give dynamics, so the knot law is my choice. A supercritical (smooth) Hopf law would keep the amplification and the flip but lose the threshold and the hysteresis.
  • The rules for bliss, learning, craving, and the antidotes life offers are one reading of the text. Different forms move the numbers in findings 3 and 6. They do not change the geometry in findings 4, 5 and 7.
  • That geometry is universal: it follows from waves plus feedback plus neighbours, and doesn’t depend on what the waves are. That makes the argument structurally sound. It is not evidence that psyches work this way. Testing that would need measurements of the kind the paper’s future-work section proposes.
  • The model makes predictions that could in principle be tested: hysteresis between attachment and release, an aim tolerance of arccos(α/2) for interventions, release that comes as a cascade rather than a slope, and release that spreads faster through a population with more variety of phase than through a synchronised one.

How the simulations run

Euler time-stepping, with the |z|⁴ term treated implicitly for stability. One psyche: seven knots, dt = 0.05, life = 600 time units. Population: a geodesic sphere of 40,962 vertices with graph-Laplacian coupling. Universe: a 48³ periodic grid (40³ on small screens). Zero-points are the triangles or grid plaquettes around which the phase winds by ±2π. Zero-lines join pierced plaquettes cube by cube. Knot seeds use the zero-set of u² − v³ (trefoil) and u² − v² (Hopf link) on the 3-sphere, projected into space. Linking numbers use the exact segment-pair Gauss integral. The figures on this page come from separate runs of the same equations in NumPy.
